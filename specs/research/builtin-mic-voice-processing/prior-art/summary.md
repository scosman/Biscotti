# Prior Art: how other macOS meeting recorders and voice apps capture the mic

## Bottom Line

Biscotti's bug (#66: our VPIO makes the call app's outgoing mic quiet or muffled) is well known in this space. At least four other recorders shipped VPIO on by default and then removed it: MacParakeet, Transcripted, OpenOats and Vext. They reported the same symptoms. Zoom participants heard the user "much quieter/muffled", "Zoom lost the user's voice", other apps' playback got quieter, and in-process capture stalled or went silent. That happened even with other-audio ducking disabled or set to `.min`.

The open-source field settled on a plain, non-VP mic tap plus a separate system-audio track. Echo is removed in software using the system track as the reference: live (anarlog/Hyprnote, Muesli, Prismical, Corti), after the recording stops (MacParakeet, Humla), or only at the transcript level (OpenOats, Vexa). AEC is skipped when the output is headphones. Level is fixed with software gain. The only VPIO uses that remain are gated: Transcripted asks for consent per meeting and never turns it on while Zoom, Teams, Webex or FaceTime is running, and TypeWhisper turns it on only for the built-in mic in its 3-channel call layout. No project or vendor documents a VPIO configuration that leaves a concurrent call unaffected. Commercial vendors that disclose their approach (Recall.ai, RecordKit, and the Granola teardown) use WebRTC AEC3 or Chromium's processing, not VPIO.

## Key Findings

- **The #66 bug, reproduced elsewhere.** MacParakeet (2026-05-14) reported: "When MacParakeet meeting recording starts, the user's voice heard by the other Zoom participant becomes much quieter/muffled. Pausing or stopping the recording restores the voice", in both directions. Ducking was already suppressed. The format was VPIO 48 kHz with 9 channels. Their fix was raw mic by default. Their hypothesis is that "VPIO AGC can write the shared hardware input gain other apps inherit"; it is untested. ([nab-feedback plan](https://github.com/moona3k/macparakeet/blob/main/plans/completed/2026-05-nab-feedback-asap-bugs.md), [echo-suppression plan](https://github.com/moona3k/macparakeet/blob/main/plans/completed/2026-05-meeting-neural-echo-suppression.md))
- **Transcripted reverted VPIO after one release.** 1.1.24 (2026-04-24) fixed a quiet mic in Safari/Firefox calls but dimmed other apps' audio. The maintainer: "It messed up a ton of meeting recording with Zoom and other apps." It now uses software AGC (up to 25×) plus a consent-gated per-meeting "Boost Mic" that turns on VPIO. A policy blocks VPIO whenever Zoom, Teams, Webex or FaceTime is running, because VPIO "fights them for the shared mic (Zoom lost the user's voice) and makes their call audio quieter". They are explicit that `.min` ducking "is the lowest level, not zero". ([#500](https://github.com/r3dbars/transcripted/issues/500), [PR #1075](https://github.com/r3dbars/transcripted/pull/1075), `CallAppMicrophoneSharingMonitor.swift`)
- **OpenOats ran VPIO for one day**, 2026-03-20 to 2026-03-21. It "conflicts with system audio capture … aggregate-device reconfiguration that can stall the mic stream". It now uses transcript text-similarity dedup only. ([PR #103](https://github.com/yazinsai/OpenOats/pull/103))
- **Vext removed VPIO.** "every other app reading the same microphone — Zoom, FaceTime, OBS … sees AGC and noise suppression applied to their feed too. The user's voice sounds distant and gain-reduced in the call." ([Vext 1.2.0 blog](https://muvon.io/blog/vext-1-2-0-two-pass-diarization-and-five-languages))
- **VPIO silenced a Core Audio process tap in the same process** (MacParakeet, macOS 15). Stale `VPAUAggregateAudioDevice` and `CADefaultDeviceAggregate-<PID>-N` devices outlived the engine. Biscotti pairs a process tap with VPIO, so the internal-evidence subtopic should check whether our system track is affected. ([vpio-process-tap-conflict.md](https://github.com/moona3k/macparakeet/blob/main/docs/research/vpio-process-tap-conflict.md))
- **VPIO's echo removal varies.** Screenpipe measured about **0 dB ERLE** with an input-only VPIO unit. Rendering the system track into VPIO's output made echo *worse*. ([#3938](https://github.com/screenpipe/screenpipe/issues/3938)) The mechanism belongs to the VPIO subtopic.
- **The anarlog/Hyprnote recipe**, the most mature OSS design, read from source: cpal raw mic averaged to mono; a global mono process tap; a live DTLN-aec-style ONNX canceller at 16 kHz, preceded by a cross-correlation reference aligner (≤600 ms) and followed by a residual linear canceller; **AEC disabled when every playing output is headphones**; the mic VAD-masked; recorder and STT both get the AEC'd mic. One open bug: AEC fragments transcripts with an external webcam mic and laptop speakers ([#4560](https://github.com/fastrepl/anarlog/issues/4560)).
- **Measured software-AEC results are mixed.** Humla's offline MDF canceller removed **17–21 dB** in a real room on an M1 Max. Screenpipe's FDAF/NLMS got **~0 dB** on real MacBook echo despite 39 dB on synthetic tests. MacParakeet's harness showed **2.5 ms misalignment destroys cancellation**, and its joint LocalVQE model zeroed near-end speech. Two more alignment measurements: Humla's mic/system offset was 125–414 ms per take, mostly start latency; Meeting Transcriber's inter-track offset was 10–20 ms. Alignment matters more than the choice of algorithm. Recall.ai says the same: "Without proper audio synchronization, acoustic echo cancellation will not work."
- **Averaging the built-in array loses the voice.** OpenOats measured peak −70 dB averaged against −7 dB on channel 0 during a call. TypeWhisper, Transcripted and Meetily switched to the strongest channel or the first 2 channels. This conflicts with Biscotti's own ch0≈ch1≈ch2≈0.004 measurement; the array subtopic should resolve it. ([PR #561](https://github.com/yazinsai/OpenOats/pull/561), [PR #755](https://github.com/TypeWhisper/typewhisper-mac/pull/755))
- **Candidate "built-in + call active" detectors** from other apps: TypeWhisper uses `transportType == BuiltIn && channelCount == 3`. Transcripted uses ~30 s of deep attenuation with speech present while AGC is pinned at max.
- **Multichannel interfaces.** Pro tools (OBS, VoiceInk, Audio Hijack) expose a user channel map via AUHAL `kAudioOutputUnitProperty_ChannelMap`. VoiceInk defaults to the device's `kAudioDevicePropertyPreferredChannelsForStereo`. `AVAudioConverter` multichannel→mono downmix silently yields zeros on non-standard layouts (TypeWhisper; Biscotti hit this too).
- **Commercial apps.** Granola, per a third-party static teardown of v7.663.2, takes the mic through Chromium `getUserMedia` with `echoCancellation: "all"` and has a dormant AUHAL + WebRTC AEC3 native path with a headphone bypass and gain compensation, not VPIO. Recall.ai's SDK and RecordKit use WebRTC AEC3 with the system track as reference. Krisp owns playback via virtual mic and speaker devices so its AEC has a reference. MacWhisper made echo cancellation default once it moved system capture to Core Audio taps. "Use headphones" is the universal documented workaround (Loom, Granola, Recall.ai, Krisp).

## Open Questions / Gaps

- **No project or vendor documents a VPIO configuration** (AGC off, ducking off, bypass, a separate process) that leaves a concurrent Zoom/Teams/Meet call unaffected. MacParakeet's AGC-off change was never tested on a live call. I found no counterexample, but absence of reports is weak evidence.
- **How Chromium implements `echoCancellation: "all"` on macOS** (VPIO or WebRTC AEC3 with a loopback reference) decides whether Granola's path could disturb calls. That is unresolved here and handed to the VPIO subtopic. The Granola teardown is static, third-party and unverified by me.
- **Whether VPIO cancels other apps' playback at all on macOS.** Screenpipe measured ~0 dB with an input-only unit. MacParakeet assumed it references the output mix. VPIO subtopic.
- **The built-in array's channel content conflicts** across Biscotti, OpenOats, TypeWhisper and Meetily. Array subtopic.
- **Several issues were not readable**: anarlog's older AEC issues #1428, #3096 and #4314 returned 404, and the screenpipe PR #4379 and cpal#4 pages came through only as summaries. Vext's blog has no visible date. `rogueamoeba.com`, `char.com` and `macwhisper-site.vercel.app` were blocked for direct fetch; I used search-engine extracts (Exa) for those pages.
- **Commercial products mostly don't disclose mic processing** (Otter, Fathom, Notion, Jamie, Bluedot, Superwhisper, Rewind/Limitless). Per the plan, I did not reverse-engineer them.
- **Real-hardware ERLE for shipped WebRTC AEC3 setups** (Screenpipe Sonora, Prismical, OpenWhispr, RecordKit) was not published. The only real-room numbers are Humla's offline MDF and Screenpipe's failed NLMS.
